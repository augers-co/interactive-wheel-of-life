How to use the Wheel of Life

← Back to the wheel

The Wheel of Life is a way of seeing your whole life at once, instead of the one part of it that happens to be loudest today. It takes about ten minutes the first time.

1. Make the categories yours

The page opens with eight common life areas. Treat them as a draft. Rename anything that doesn't fit your vocabulary, delete what doesn't apply, and add what's missing.

  • Click any name to edit it.
  • + Add area creates a new slice; the × removes one.
  • Split a category when one label is hiding two different things. If "Health" is really "sleep" and "training", and those are in very different shape, they deserve separate slices.
  • Drag the handle at the left of a row to reorder. Slices sit on the wheel in list order, so you can put related areas side by side.

Somewhere between six and twelve areas usually works. Fewer and each slice is too broad to act on; many more and the exercise turns into admin.

2. Score each area as it is today

Move each slider to how satisfied you are with that part of your life right now — not how satisfied you think you ought to be, and not where you're aiming to get to. 0 is the centre of the wheel, 100 is the outer edge.

Go with your first instinct on each one. The number you land on in three seconds is usually more honest than the one you argue yourself into over three minutes.

A useful calibration: 50 is not "average", it's genuinely mixed. Reserve the top of the range for areas you'd change nothing about.

3. Write down why

Open the note under any area with the chevron. A score on its own fades; the sentence explaining it is what you'll actually want when you come back to this.

You don't need to fill in all of them. The two or three that surprised you are the ones worth a note.

4. Read the shape, not the numbers

Step back and look at the outline. A wheel that reaches evenly outward rolls; one with a few long slices and a few stubs doesn't, however impressive the long ones are.

Sit with these:

  • Which slice is furthest from where you'd want it?
  • Is any high score being paid for by a low one next to it?
  • If one area moved ten points, which would make the most difference to the rest?
  • Which low score are you genuinely at peace with? Not everything has to be high — a deliberate trade-off is not the same as neglect.

Resist the urge to fix everything. One area, one concrete next step, is a better outcome than eight resolutions.

5. Save it, then come back

Press Save snapshot to keep a dated copy. The real value of this exercise shows up the second time you do it.

When you return — a month, a quarter, whenever — adjust the sliders to today's reality and press Compare on the old snapshot. The wheel draws a line across each slice showing where it stood then, and every row gains a ▲ or ▼ against the old score. Movement you'd never have recalled becomes obvious.

Taking it into a session

Print opens a clean copy with every note expanded, which saves as a PDF from the print dialog. Download PNG gives you a dated image of just the wheel, which is easier to drop into a message or a journal.

Where your answers live

Nothing here is uploaded. There's no account, no server, and the page makes no network requests — it works with the wifi switched off. Your scores, notes and snapshots are stored by your browser, on the device you're using.

Two consequences worth knowing:

  • Your wheel won't follow you to a different browser or computer.
  • Clearing your browser's site data erases it.

If you intend to compare wheels months apart, use Export backup to save everything to a single file you keep yourself. Import loads it back.

A note for coaches

Everything is in one HTML file with no build step and no dependencies. Fork it, edit the DEFAULTS list at the top of the script to your own categories, and hand the file to clients. Because their answers never leave their own machine, there's nothing for you to host or safeguard.

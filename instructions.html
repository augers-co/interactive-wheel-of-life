How to use the Wheel of Life

← Back to the wheel

The Wheel of Life is used to clarify priorities before setting goals, so that you can plan towards whatever balance means for you.

Balance is personal. An arrangement that one person finds balanced can feel stressful, or dull, to someone else. There is no correct wheel, and no shape you are supposed to be aiming at.

It is also worth assessing more than once. Repeating the exercise at intervals shows patterns that a single sitting won't.

1. Put your name on it (optional)

There is a name field under the title. It is only used on the printed copy and in the filename when you export, so that a wheel you hand to someone is identifiable. Leave it blank and nothing appears.

2. Review the areas

The wheel opens with eight areas. Taken together, they should add up to a picture of a balanced life for you. Adjust them until they do:

  • Click any name to re-label it in words that mean something to you.
  • Split an area in two if it is covering something that deserves its own space.
  • + Add area adds one; × removes one.
  • Drag the handle at the left of a row to reorder. Areas sit on the wheel in list order. The keyboard works too — focus a handle and use ↑/↓.
  • Click the coloured dot beside an area to change its colour. Each area starts with one picked to be distinct from its neighbours, so you only need to do this if a particular colour means something to you. Reset colours puts them all back.
  • Buttons that throw something away — Reset, Restore, Delete, Import — ask first: click once and the button turns into a question, click again to go ahead. Press Escape or click elsewhere to back out.

Areas that commonly appear, if you want alternatives to the defaults:

  • Family / friends
  • Partner / significant other / romance
  • Career
  • Finances
  • Health — emotional, physical, fitness, nutrition, wellbeing
  • Environment / home
  • Fun / recreation / leisure
  • Personal growth / learning / self-development
  • Spiritual wellbeing — not necessarily religion; it can mean a sense of self
  • Security
  • Service
  • Leadership
  • Integrity
  • Achievement
  • Community

3. Score each area

Consider what success would feel like in each area. Then rate how satisfied you currently are with that part of your life, from 1 (very dissatisfied) to 10 (fully satisfied).

Each slice reaches out in proportion to its score, so a 5 sits half way to the outer edge. Drag the slider or type into the number box; it moves in tenths, so 7.4 is available if 7 and 8 both feel wrong.

4. Add context (optional)

Each area has an optional note, collapsed by default — open it with the chevron if you want to record anything alongside the number. Leaving them all empty is fine; empty notes are left out when you print.

5. Look at the whole wheel

The outer edge of the coloured area is your wheel as it currently stands; the pale remainder of each slice is the distance to 10. Some questions to take the exercise further:

  • Are there any surprises here?
  • How do you feel about your life as you look at it?
  • How do you currently spend your time across these areas?
  • How would you like to spend your time across them?
  • Which of these would you most like to improve?
  • What would a 10 look like in that area? What would make it a 10?
  • How could you make space for those changes?
  • Can you make them on your own, or is there help and cooperation you would need from other people?

6. Write an action plan

While the wheel is fresh, note down three things you will actually do in the area or areas you feel most strongly about. Use the Action plan section below the areas. It starts with three rows; add more with + Add action, or delete any of them with × — the whole section is optional.

They can be large or small — anything from starting something new to making a regular phone call. What matters is that they are things you will do rather than things you would like to be true.

7. Save it and return

Save snapshot keeps a dated copy of the whole wheel, including your notes and actions, in the History section further down. When you come back, update the sliders and press Compare on the earlier snapshot: the wheel draws a line across each slice at the score it held then, and each row shows the change.

Snapshots live in this browser. To keep them somewhere safer, or to move them to another computer, see below.

Getting an outside view

You can also ask someone who knows you well to score the wheel as they see you, which can be a useful comparison against your own scoring. If you do, choose someone you trust and whose opinion you actually value.

Sharing it with a coach

There are two ways to get your wheel out of the browser, and they do different jobs.

Save as PDF is the one to send if someone just needs to read it. It downloads a finished PDF straight away — no print dialog, and none of the page headers and URLs a browser adds when you print. Inside is your name and the date, a full-size wheel, every area with its score and note, and your action plan. Empty notes and blank actions are left out.

Export file, at the top of the History section, writes everything — areas, scores, notes, actions and every snapshot — to a single file. This is the one worth keeping. Send it to your coach and they can use Import file to open your wheel in their own copy of this page and work with it live. Import replaces whatever is currently on their page, so it is worth exporting their own wheel first if they have one.

Put simply: the PDF is for reading, the exported file is for carrying on with.

Where your answers live

Nothing here is uploaded. There is no account and no server, and the page makes no network requests — it works with the wifi switched off. Your scores, notes, actions and snapshots are stored by your browser, on the device you are using.

  • Your wheel will not follow you to a different browser or computer.
  • Clearing your browser's site data erases it.

If you intend to compare wheels months apart, use Export file to save everything somewhere you control. Import file loads it back — on this computer or another one.

Sharing a computer

Because everything is stored by the browser rather than by a server, one person's wheel can never appear on someone else's machine. Two people sharing the same browser on the same computer are sharing that storage, though, and the second person would open the page and find the first person's answers.

Clear this device, in the History section, is the way out: it erases the current wheel, every snapshot, the name and the action plan from this browser. It cannot be undone, so export first if you want to keep any of it. A private or incognito window is the other option — nothing survives once you close it.

For coaches

This is one HTML file with no build step and no dependencies. Fork it, edit the DEFAULTS list at the top of the script to your own areas, and give the file to clients. Because their answers stay on their own machine, there is nothing for you to host or safeguard.
